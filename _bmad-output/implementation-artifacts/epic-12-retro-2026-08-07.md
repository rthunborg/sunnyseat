## 2. What Went Well

- **All 14 planned stories landed without scope evaporation.** Epic 12 did not quietly drop the difficult stories; it carried the full trust/persistence/media/search sweep through to implemented code and verification artifacts.
- **Shared product contracts were centralized instead of being re-litigated story-by-story.** Hours, geometry identity, public venue visibility, sunny/not-sunny truth, exact-name closed-search behavior, and media rendition semantics all ended the epic as explicit seams rather than tribal knowledge.
- **The team stayed honest about visual and launch evidence.** Manual/providerless visual approval was used when credentials were unavailable, but references were not self-blessed; human approval remained explicit, and protected/live evidence gaps were documented instead of papered over.
- **Automation quality improved materially even while the epic widened scope.** A11y-mobile coverage, broad Vitest sweeps, full Playwright runs, migration replay checks, and whole-epic review/test-gate passes all became part of the normal finish line rather than optional extras.
- **Whole-epic review still paid for itself after story-local reviews were complete.** The epic integration review surfaced cross-story defects and policy gaps that individual story loops had missed, and those survivors were patched before this retro rather than being converted into a vague future debt pile.
- **The epic refused to call local green "launch-ready."** That sounds basic, but it is the right behavior: the release-blocking evidence gaps are operational and protected-environment-specific, and the epic recorded that distinction clearly instead of conflating implementation completion with production proof.

## 3. What Was Hard

- **Protected/live evidence lanes were not satisfiable from ordinary story execution.** Story 12.3, 12.7, and 12.12 all reached strong local proof, but the remaining launch-critical questions require protected GitHub/Supabase environments or production-only evidence that this delegate session could not obtain.
- **Epic/story prose drift repeatedly had to be corrected in-flight.** Several stories required explicit supersession controls because the retained epic wording no longer matched the resolved product/architecture decision by the time implementation actually happened.
- **Windows-host and provider-friction kept showing up at the edges.** `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` availability, visual-validator credential gating, `CI=1` for Playwright-owned servers, worker caps for large Vitest runs, and occasional `spawn EPERM`/full-suite timing issues all added operational drag.
- **Mocks could conceal live-schema truth.** Story 12.7 is the clearest example: local tests and mocks could look correct while the real migrated visibility contract was still unproven against the live schema branch.
- **Convergence under concurrency stayed harder than correctness under isolation.** 12.6 needed explicit marker-convergence diagnostics, 12.7 still has a concurrent visibility/cache evidence gap, and 12.11's future-date exact-response wait can still time out under full-suite concurrency even when the visible UI is already on "tomorrow."

## 4. Key Insights

1. **Implementation-complete and launch-ready are different states and must stay different in the tracker.** Epic 12 landed all planned story code, yet the correct epic verdict is still "not launch-ready" because the protected/live evidence lanes are incomplete.
2. **Protected-environment requirements need preflight treatment, not end-of-epic discovery.** The epic knew some stories depended on live or protected proof, but the remaining blockers show that those lanes should be staged explicitly before the final gate rather than discovered at the boundary.
3. **Explicit shared contracts reduce product drift.** The stories that went best are the ones that ratified one truth seam for everyone else to reuse: hours, geometry identity, public sun state, venue visibility, and closed-search handling.
4. **Manual visual acceptance can still be rigorous if the approval boundary stays explicit.** Epic 12 used providerless/manual visual flows several times without collapsing into "close enough" because every approval remained auditable and separately authorized.
5. **Concurrency/convergence evidence deserves its own gate language.** A feature can be logically correct and still not be fully releasable if the "all markers converge," "future-date response stays aligned," or "concurrent cache invalidation behaves under overlap" lanes remain partial.

## 9. Planning Drift

Epic 12 had meaningful planning drift, but most of it was handled explicitly instead of by silent reinterpretation:

- **Structural drift — Story 12.1:** the original "Google Places opening-hours sync" framing was already obsolete by implementation time. The real story was a provider-neutral, independently sourced weekly-hours workflow, and the title had to be treated as historical.
- **Structural drift — Story 12.10:** older epic wording implied a broader/more opportunistic prefetch model. The maintained decision was narrower and more defensible: initial-settle-only, six candidates, concurrency two, no scrub/date restart, no 10 km expansion.
- **Structural drift — Story 12.14:** the original closed-venue policy was superseded by the resolved exact-name/favourites rule: closed exact-name matches stay discoverable, and closed favourites remain openable in a greyed state.
- **Product-copy drift — Stories 12.8 and 12.13:** older materials still implied public confidence/accuracy exposition even after the product decision moved confidence internal-only. The epic had to strip or reinterpret that stale framing.
- **Execution drift — Story 12.7:** mocks and local assumptions were ahead of live migrated truth. The epic correctly landed the code seam, but the planning assumption that code-level correctness would also prove live visibility behavior turned out false.
- **Operational drift — epic boundary:** planning implicitly treated launch evidence as a story-local finish condition, but in practice the protected/live lanes became epic-boundary work items. That is the biggest process lesson to carry forward.
